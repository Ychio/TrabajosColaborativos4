Abstract. Recent pulsar timing array (PTA) observations have reported evidence for a com-
mon low frequency signal consistent with a stochastic gravitational wave background. In
this work, we investigate the cosmic string interpretation of this signal in a non-standard
cosmological history between big bang nucleosynthesis and recombination, featuring an early
matter-dominated era followed by a kination phase. Previous studies have shown that kination
can significantly modify the cosmic-string-induced gravitational wave spectrum, generating
characteristic features in the nanohertz range and producing a shape consistent with current
PTA data. Building on this, we perform separate Bayesian analyses of the NANOGrav 15-year
and EPTA DR2 New+ datasets to quantify the extent to which such a cosmological history is
favored by the data. We find substantial evidence for a transient period of matter and kina-
tion domination over the standard cosmological history within a cosmic-string interpretation
of the gravitational-wave signal, with a Bayes factor 3.63+0.22

−0.21 from the NANOGrav 15-year
dataset, while leaving the inferred cosmic-string tension nearly unchanged. This cosmic-
string-dominated scenario is particularly relevant if the contribution from supermassive black
hole binaries (SMBHBs) is small, as hinted at by astrophysical population simulations. Com-
pared with a scenario where gravitational waves are produced purely by SMBHBs, cosmic-
string interpretations with and without an additional contribution from SMBHBs, across both
expansion histories and datasets, reveal no significant preference.

1 Introduction

The growing sensitivity of pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) has opened a new observational win-
dow onto the nanohertz gravitational wave (GW) regime. Recent results by the NANOGrav
15-year collaboration [1], the European Pulsar Timing Array (EPTA) [2], the Parkes Pulsar
Timing Array (PPTA) [3] and the Chinese Pulsar Timing Array (CPTA) [4] have reported
evidence for a stochastic GW background (SGWB) in the nanohertz regime. These develop-
ments have generated considerable interest since the PTA signal may provide a direct probe
not only of astrophysical sources such as supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHBs), but
also of relic processes that may have occurred in the early Universe. Possible early-Universe
cosmological sources relevant to the PTA band include cosmic strings (CS) [5, 6], first-order
phase transitions [7, 8], scalar-induced gravitational waves [9, 10], and gravitational waves
associated with non-standard cosmological histories such as kination [11, 12].

In the early Universe, cosmic strings can form during the symmetry breaking phase
transition via the Kibble mechanism [13, 14]. Depending on whether the underlying sponta-
neously broken symmetry is global or gauged, cosmic strings are broadly classified into global

– 1 –



and local strings. In this work, we focus on local cosmic strings. Such symmetry-breaking
patterns arise in a variety of particle physics models. A common example is the breaking
of an extra Abelian gauge symmetry such as U(1)B−L, which frequently appears in models
for neutrino mass generation based on the seesaw mechanism [15, 16]. Local strings may
also emerge in grand unified theories whose symmetry-breaking pattern contains an inter-
mediate U(1) symmetry, as in various SO(10) constructions [17, 18]. These scenarios make
local cosmic strings theoretically and phenomenologically well motivated. Once formed, they
follow a scaling law where the fraction of energy density in the network compared to the
background is fixed. Moreover, on scales much larger than their core width, local strings
are often well approximated by Nambu–Goto strings, so their network evolution leads to
predictive cosmological signatures, most notably an SGWB produced by oscillating cosmic
string loops [5, 19–21]. The resulting SGWB depends not only on string parameters such as
the tension, emission spectrum, and intercommutation probability, but also heavily on the
background expansion history of the Universe [11]. Therefore, the GW spectrum generated
by cosmic strings can reveal information not only about string microphysics but also about
the history of the cosmological evolution.

Among the simplest and well-motivated departures from the standard thermal history
is a kination-dominated (KD) era, during which the energy density of the Universe is domi-
nated by the kinetic energy of a scalar field [22, 23]. A particularly natural realization arises
in theories containing a complex scalar field with a global U(1) symmetry and a scalar poten-
tial whose non-zero vacuum expectation value spontaneously breaks the U(1) symmetry. In
addition, higher-dimensional operators explicitly break the symmetry and, for a large initial
radial field value, provide a kick in the angular direction that initiates field rotation in the
early Universe. While the radial mode is still displaced from its vacuum value, the scalar-field
energy can drive an early matter-dominated (MD) phase. As the radial mode thermalizes,
part of the energy stored in the complex scalar field is dissipated, while the approximately
conserved charge prevents the energy from being completely depleted. The motion then ap-
proaches circular rotation in field space. Once the radial mode settles to the vacuum, the
potential energy becomes negligible. In this regime, the energy density is dominated by rota-
tional kinetic energy, giving rise to a kination era. This kination era corresponds to a phase
with an equation of state parameter w ≃ 1, where the energy density redshifts as a−6 with a
the scale factor. Since this component redshifts faster than radiation, the kination phase is
temporary and the Universe eventually returns to the radiation-dominated (RD) epoch.

The PTA signal is also interesting from the viewpoint of non-standard expansion histo-
ries. Previous work has shown that if an early matter-dominated era is followed by kination,
with both transitions occurring between big bang nucleosynthesis (BBN) and recombina-
tion, the SGWB from cosmic strings can deviate substantially from the nearly flat spectrum
expected in the standard radiation-dominated cosmology [11]. In particular, this late matter–
kination sequence can modify the resulting spectral structure in the nanohertz range. Mo-
tivated by this possibility, in this work we perform a Bayesian analysis of the cosmic string
interpretation in the presence of such a late matter-to-kination domination history, and assess
whether it provides a better description of current PTA data than the standard cosmology.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec. 2, we review axion rotation
that can give rise to the kination cosmology considered in this work, characterized by an
early matter-dominated era followed by a kination-dominated era. In Sec. 3, we describe
GW production from local cosmic strings and detail the resulting spectrum in the presence
of the modified expansion history. In Sec. 4, we outline the Bayesian analysis framework and

describe the use of Bayes factors for model comparison. In Sec. 5, we present the constraints
imposed by BBN and cosmic microwave background (CMB) observations. The results of the
PTA analyses are presented and discussed in Sec. 6, followed by our conclusions in Sec. 7.

the U(1) symmetry, it dissipates energy without affecting the associated charge, i.e., rotation
in the angular direction. Thermalization therefore does not remove the entire energy stored
in the complex scalar field. Rather, it removes the energy associated with radial motion,
rendering the rotational motion circular.

2.3 Characteristic temperatures

In this work, both MD and KD are essential in producing the characteristic cosmic string
GW spectrum that is relevant for PTA observations. For this cosmological sequence to occur
between BBN and recombination, the radial mode should thermalize before the energy density
stored in the rotating condensate dominates over the radiation energy density. If this criterion
is satisfied, the Universe enters an early MD era and transitions to a KD era. As a result,
the cosmological history naturally proceeds from being RD to MD, to KD, and subsequently
to RD. This cosmology is illustrated schematically in Fig. 1. Importantly, neither transition
requires the dominant component to decay into radiation. In particular, unlike the usual early

by entropy production. The eventual transition from MD to KD and then to RD occurs
automatically through cosmic redshift.

3 Cosmic string gravitational waves

3.1 Local cosmic strings and their gravitational wave background

Local cosmic strings can form when a U(1) gauge symmetry is spontaneously broken [13]. In
local strings, unlike global strings, the would-be Goldstone mode is eaten by the gauge field,

3.3 GW spectrum modified by matter and kination eras

The important point for the present work is that cosmic strings are long lasting sources of
GW. Because of this, the observed frequency today carries information about both the epoch
when the relevant loops were formed and the epoch when they emit GW. Any non-standard
expansion can leave an imprint on the string induced SGWB, e.g., Refs. [43–46].

shape. If we neglect the sum over higher harmonics, one finds the following asymptotic

into a peaked structure around the matter-to-kination transition.

5 Analyses

In this section, we describe the setup adopted for the Bayesian analysis. Our main goal is to
determine how well the cosmic string induced SGWB can fit PTA data in the presence of an
intermediate non-standard cosmological era. To address this question, we consider a modified
expansion history between BBN and recombination, following the sequence

RD → MD → KD → RD.

As discussed in Sec. 3, such a non-standard history deforms the broad cosmic string spectrum
into a broken power-law shape with a characteristic bump. If the transition scales are between
BBN and recombination, this feature can be shifted into the nanohertz frequency range probed
by PTA experiments, where it may provide a better fit to the observed signal than the
prediction from standard cosmology. This motivates the study of not only the PTA fit, but
also whether the corresponding non-standard era remains consistent with BBN and the CMB.

In realistic UV complete models [24], the transitions between these epochs are smooth.
In the present analysis, however, we adopt a simplified description in which the background
evolution is treated piecewise with instantaneous transitions in the scaling of the scalar field’s
energy density with respect to the scale factor. This approximation captures the leading

impact of the non-standard era on the cosmic string gravitational wave spectrum while sim-
plifying the numerical calculation for the Bayesian analysis. The expansion history is therefore
specified by the characteristic transition temperatures TRM and TKR introduced in the previ-

the duration and timing of the MD and KD, and hence control the corresponding deformation
of the cosmic string spectrum relevant for PTA observations. Consequently, we compute the

5.1 Constraints

Our inference proceeds in three stages. The non-standard expansion history is parametrized
by the radiation-to-matter and kination-to-radiation transition temperatures, TRM and TKR,
with TMK determined by Eq. (2.19). First, we use BBN light-element abundances to constrain
TRM and construct its marginalized posterior distribution. We then fix TRM to the 2σ BBN
upper bound, together with the corresponding helium abundance, and use CMB and baryon
acoustic oscillations (BAO) observations to constrain TKR. The prior range for TKR is chosen
to ensure that the Universe returns to radiation domination before recombination while main-
taining the required ordering TKR ≤ TRM. As shown below, the inferred TKR distribution is
largely insensitive to variations of TRM within its BBN-allowed range, motivating the separate
BBN and CMB+BAO fits. Finally, kernel-density estimates of the marginalized BBN and
CMB+BAO posteriors are used as informative priors for TRM and TKR, respectively, in the
PTA analysis, where both transition temperatures are varied together with the cosmic-string
and astrophysical parameters. Thus, BBN and CMB+BAO first determine the allowed non-
standard expansion histories, while the NANOGrav and EPTA data subsequently test their
imprint on the gravitational-wave spectrum.

7 Conclusion and outlook

Our primary objective here is to investigate how a non-standard expansion history featuring
an early matter-dominated era followed by a kination phase between BBN and recombination
can leave characteristic imprints on the SGWB sourced by cosmic strings in the nanohertz
regime probed by NG15 and EPTA DR2 New+ pulsar timing array datasets. We perform
a comprehensive Bayesian analysis and model comparison for cosmic strings in both ΛCDM
and kination cosmologies, with and without additional SMBHB contributions.

Our analysis finds no strong evidence favoring any of the alternative hypotheses over
the SMBHB-only baseline H0, in either dataset. The underperformance of the cosmic-string
model in describing NG15 arises from the preference for a blue-tilted ΩGWh2 spectrum. A blue
tilt can be accommodated within the cosmic-string framework by moving toward smaller string
tensions, but the resulting reduction in the overall amplitude makes it difficult to account for
the large amplitude inferred by the dataset. Consequently, the cosmic-string spectrum in
ΛCDM is either too weak or too flat to simultaneously account for the inferred amplitude
and spectral tilt. The kination scenario considered in this work enhances the SGWB spectrum
toward the high-frequency end, resulting in a more pronounced blue tilt and thereby providing
a better fit compared to the counterpart in ΛCDM. In contrast to NG15, the EPTA DR2
New+ data slightly favor a flatter SGWB spectrum ΩGWh2, which is readily accommodated
by larger cosmic-string tension. This is consistent with the higher median values of log10Gµ

– 27 –



inferred from EPTA relative to NG15 across four alternative models. Nonetheless, within
the cosmic string framework, with or without contributions from SMBHBs, both datasets
mildly favor kination over ΛCDM, with the strength of preference ranging from negligibly
small in EPTA to substantial for the cosmic-string-only model in NG15. In spite of the
differences in evidence, the inferred cosmic-string-tension posteriors remain largely consistent
between the two cosmologies. The ΩGWh2 credible intervals also show substantial overlap
across the frequency range probed by NG15 and EPTA DR2 New+. We also show that
the current EPTA DR2 New+ dataset does not provide sufficient sensitivity to distinguish
the kination and ΛCDM expansion histories within the cosmic-string framework. The NG15
dataset shows a stronger preference for kination with the cosmic-string-only model providing
substantial evidence lnBNG15

31 = 1.29 ± 0.06. This preference diminishes once SMBHBs are
included.

A joint analysis of the NG15 and EPTA DR2 NEW+ datasets could, in principle, im-
prove the statistical sensitivity and provide tighter constraints on model parameters and
model evidences. However, the two PTA datasets contain overlapping pulsars; combining the
two likelihoods will double count information associated with the common pulsars, potentially
biasing the model constraints and model evidences. A statistically consistent joint analysis
will therefore require dedicated statistical treatments of the correlations between overlapping
datasets. The forthcoming International Pulsar Timing Array Third Data Release (IPTA
DR3) [89] will combine observations from multiple PTA collaborations and will provide a
more comprehensive joint dataset for characterizing the SGWB. This is particularly relevant
for the present work since NG15 and EPTA DR2 New+ exhibit different spectral preferences
and yield somewhat different statistical supports for kination cosmology. A consistent joint
analysis will clarify the nature of the SGWB and its implication for the early expansion
history, which we leave to future work.
